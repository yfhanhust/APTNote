Phase Five: Alternative Currencies and DeFis
After successfully hacking into a financial target, operatives must even­
tually convert cryptocurrency into real currency and launder it to avoid
detection. Cybersecurity experts have identified North Korean hackers
moving money through hundreds or thousands of separate transactions
in numerous countries to launder it before cashing out.
In 2019, North Korea began to use decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms
and decentralized cryptocurrency exchanges (DEX) to launder cryptocur­
rency. The Lazarus group’s use of DeFi platforms nearly doubled in 2020
as its use of mainstream cryptocurrency exchanges decreased. [173] The
shift reflects North Korea’s money-laundering adaptability in response
to increasing security protocols or law enforcement focus on existing
platforms. CipherTrace, a crypto intelligence company, assessed that
cryptocurrency thefts and hacks declined in 2020 due to increased security
procedures but that hacks against DeFis increased. [174]
Phase Six: Pharmaceutical Companies
North Korea took advantage of the COVID-19 crisis to target pharma­
ceutical companies for proprietary information on vaccine production as
well as individuals and businesses seeking COVID relief funding.
In 2020, the Lazarus group planned a large-scale phishing campaign
against more than 5 million individuals and businesses in India, Japan,
Singapore, South Korea, the U.K., and the United States. The hackers would
pose as local authorities dispersing government COVID support funds and
direct the targets to fake websites where they would divulge personal and
financial information. [175]

### Overview of North Korean Cyberattacks

North Korea's cyber operations have evolved significantly over the years, demonstrating a high level of sophistication and adaptability. These attacks can be categorized into six distinct phases, each reflecting the regime's strategic priorities and technological advancements.

#### Phase One: Intellectual Property and Data Theft (2014-2016)
**Key Targets:**
- **Sony Pictures Entertainment**: A major hack in 2014 that leaked sensitive internal documents and emails.
- **Operation Blockbuster**: A global campaign targeting defense contractors, financial institutions, and media companies.

**Objectives:**
- To steal intellectual property and sensitive information.
- To disrupt operations and cause reputational damage.

#### Phase Two: Espionage and Data Collection (2016-2018)
**Key Targets:**
- **Operation GhostSecret**: A widespread campaign targeting various sectors, including government agencies and private companies.
- **DarkSeoul**: Attacks on South Korean media and financial institutions.

**Objectives:**
- To gather intelligence for strategic decision-making.
- To disrupt critical infrastructure and create chaos.

#### Phase Three: Financial Theft (2017-2019)
**Key Targets:**
- **Central Banks**: Multiple attempts to steal funds from central banks, including the Bangladesh Bank heist in 2016.
- **ATM Networks**: The FASTCash campaign targeting ATMs in multiple countries.

**Objectives:**
- To generate revenue for the North Korean regime.
- To undermine financial stability and confidence in banking systems.

#### Phase Four: Cryptocurrency Exchanges (2017-Present)
**Key Targets:**
- **Bithumb, Youbit, Monero, Coinis, NiceHash**: Multiple attacks on cryptocurrency exchanges resulting in significant losses.
- **Operation AppleJeus**: A sophisticated campaign to deliver malware through fake cryptocurrency applications.

**Objectives:**
- To exploit the anonymity and decentralized nature of cryptocurrencies.
- To launder stolen funds and evade international sanctions.

#### Phase Five: Alternative Currencies and DeFis (2019-Present)
**Key Targets:**
- **Decentralized Finance (DeFi) Platforms**: Use of DeFi platforms to launder cryptocurrency.
- **Decentralized Exchanges (DEX)**: Shifting from mainstream exchanges to DEXs for greater anonymity.

**Objectives:**
- To adapt to increasing security measures in traditional financial systems.
- To maintain a low profile and avoid detection by law enforcement.

#### Phase Six: Pharmaceutical Companies (2020-Present)
**Key Targets:**
- **Pharmaceutical Companies**: Targeting companies working on COVID-19 vaccines and treatments.
- **Government Agencies**: Attacks on agencies conducting COVID-19 research, such as the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.

**Objectives:**
- To gain proprietary information on vaccine production.
- To disrupt global efforts to combat the pandemic.

### Recent Developments (2020-2021)
- **Phishing Campaigns**: Large-scale phishing campaigns targeting individuals and businesses seeking COVID relief funding.
- **Targeting Pfizer**: Intelligence reports suggest North Korea targeted Pfizer for COVID-19 vaccine information, though the success of these attacks is uncertain.

### Conclusion
North Korea's cyber operations continue to pose a significant threat to global security and financial stability. The regime's ability to adapt and innovate in its cyber tactics underscores the need for robust cybersecurity measures and international cooperation to counter these threats effectively.
