This report provides INTERPOL’s insights into key cyberthreats that are affecting the African region. It
also presents in-depth analysis on the impact of these threats as well as examples of INTERPOL’s
operational support in combating cybercrime and the regional cybercrime strategy for Africa. Based
on input from the member countries in Africa and data drawn from INTERPOL’s private partners, the
most prominent threats were identified as below.

Online Scams – For African member countries, the highest-reported and most pressing

cyberthreat across the region was identified as online scamming. This threat seeks to target
and take advantage of victims’ fears, insecurities, and vulnerabilities through phishing, mass
mailing and social engineering. Member countries have reported a sharp increase in the
number of online banking scams, including instances of banking and credit card fraud.
 - Digital Extortion – This threat is also identified as one of the most prominent cyberthreats
within the region. Digital extortion seeks to target individuals with either allegations of sexually
compromising images or through direct blackmail campaigns. While such threats are not new
on the threat landscape, the move towards a digital society – particularly within the African
region – has created new attack vectors for criminals to both obfuscate their identity and
target new victims.
 - Business Email Compromise – Alongside online scams, Business Email Compromise (BEC) was
identified as a significant concern and threat to the region. Businesses and organizations that
rely heavily on wire transfer transactions are vulnerable to this threat in Africa. The COVID-19
pandemic has contributed to the increase in this type of cybercrime.
 - Ransomware – The threat of ransomware is expanding across the African continent. Allegedly,
more than 61% of companies in this region were affected by ransomware in 2020 alone. [1] These
attacks targeted some African countries’ critical infrastructure, including healthcare and
maritime sectors. [2]

Botnets – Botnets are networks of compromised machines used as a tool to automate large
scale campaigns such as DDoS attacks, phishing, malware distribution, etc. The number of
botnet victim detections in Africa was around 50,000, with a monthly average detection of
3,900. In Africa, there have been numerous high-profile instances of such DDoS attacks on
critical infrastructure within the past five years.

Recognizing the need for a change in the approach to cybercrime within Africa as a region that is
embracing digital transformation, the report concludes with INTERPOL’s regional cybercrime strategy
to support member countries in Africa. The strategy encompasses the four strategic objectives below:

Enhancing cybercrime intelligence for effective responses to cybercrime ;
Strengthening cooperation for joint operations against cybercrime;
Developing regional capacity and capabilities to combat cybercrime;
Promoting good cyber hygiene for a safer cyberspace.

INTERPOL stands ready to support African member countries in fulfilling these objectives and further
developing a joint operational framework to improve coordinated actions against cybercrime in Africa.
Collective efforts in sharing intelligence and formulating a joint operational framework will boost
regional capabilities and capacity in the fight against cybercrime.

### 2.2 Digital Extortion

Digital extortion is a significant cyber threat that involves threat actors using various tactics to extort money from victims. This can include blackmail, sextortion, and ransomware attacks. The goal is to coerce individuals or organizations into paying a sum of money in exchange for the threat actor ceasing their malicious activities.

#### Types of Digital Extortion

1. **Blackmail:**
   - **Description:** Threat actors threaten to release sensitive information or embarrassing content unless a payment is made.
   - **Common Tactics:**
     - Stealing personal data through phishing attacks, malware, or social engineering.
     - Leveraging publicly available information from data breaches.
     - Using social media and other online platforms to gather personal details.

2. **Sextortion:**
   - **Description:** Threat actors claim to have intimate or compromising images or videos of the victim and demand payment to prevent their release.
   - **Common Tactics:**
     - Hacking into webcams or using malware to capture private moments.
     - Using previously leaked data from other breaches to create a semblance of authenticity.
     - Sending generic threats that may not be specific to the victim but are designed to instill fear.

3. **Ransomware:**
   - **Description:** Malware that encrypts the victim's files and demands a ransom in exchange for the decryption key.
   - **Common Tactics:**
     - Spreading via email attachments, malicious downloads, or exploiting vulnerabilities in software.
     - Targeting both individuals and organizations, including critical infrastructure.
     - Using sophisticated encryption methods to make it difficult to recover files without paying the ransom.

#### Situation in Africa

Digital extortion is a growing concern across African countries. The combination of increasing internet penetration, limited cybersecurity awareness, and economic challenges makes many individuals and organizations vulnerable targets.

- **Ransomware Attacks:**
  - According to a report by Kaspersky, there has been a significant increase in ransomware attacks targeting African businesses and institutions. [21]
  - Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) are particularly at risk due to their limited resources for robust cybersecurity measures.

- **Blackmail and Sextortion:**
  - These forms of digital extortion often target individuals, leveraging social media and dating apps to gather personal information.
  - There have been several high-profile cases in countries like South Africa and Nigeria where celebrities and public figures have been victims of sextortion attempts.

#### Case Studies

1. **Ransomware Attack on a South African Hospital:**
   - In 2020, a major hospital in South Africa fell victim to a ransomware attack that encrypted patient records and disrupted critical services.
   - The attackers demanded a significant ransom in Bitcoin to restore access to the systems.

2. **Sextortion Campaign Targeting University Students:**
   - A widespread sextortion campaign targeted students at several universities across Nigeria.
   - Threat actors claimed to have recorded intimate videos of the students and demanded payments to prevent their release on social media.

#### Mitigation Strategies

1. **User Education:**
   - Conduct regular cybersecurity awareness training for employees and individuals.
   - Teach users how to identify phishing emails, suspicious links, and other common tactics used by threat actors.

2. **Technical Measures:**
   - Implement strong firewalls, anti-malware software, and intrusion detection systems.
   - Regularly update and patch software to close known vulnerabilities.

3. **Data Backup:**
   - Maintain regular backups of important data and store them securely offline.
   - Test backup restoration procedures to ensure they work effectively in case of a ransomware attack.

4. **Legal Action:**
   - Report incidents to law enforcement agencies and cooperate with investigations.
   - Consider legal action against perpetrators, although this can be challenging due to the anonymity provided by the internet.

By combining these strategies, African countries can better protect their citizens and organizations from the growing threat of digital extortion.
