### Summary of Key Points from the Hi-Tech Crime Trends Report (2019/2020)

#### **General Overview:**
The report highlights significant trends and threats in the realm of cybercrime, particularly focusing on targeted attacks, financial sector vulnerabilities, and evolving techniques used by threat actors.

#### **Targeted Attacks:**
- **Telecommunications and Financial Institutions:** Increased focus on telecommunications and financial institutions.
- **State-Sponsored Actors:** Growing involvement of state-sponsored groups targeting critical infrastructure.
- **Ransomware:** Notable increase in ransomware attacks, with demands often exceeding $100,000.

#### **Financial Sector:**
- **Targeted Attacks:**
  - **Banking Trojans for PC and Android:** Decline in the creation of new banking Trojans by Russian-speaking hackers. Trickbot has significantly evolved with new modules.
  - **Fileless Attacks:** Increased use of fileless attacks and tools like Mimikatz to steal credentials.
  - **Social Engineering:** Rise in sophisticated social engineering techniques, including remote control tool installations on mobile devices.

- **Non-Targeted Attacks:**
  - **Carding Activities:** Significant growth due to the spread of JS sniffers. Dumps continue to have a large share of the carding market.
  - **JS Sniffers:** Became a major point of growth for textual data theft. More JS sniffers are now in use than banking Trojans.
  - **Phishing and Social Engineering:** Expected decline in traditional phishing, with an explosive growth in social engineering techniques.

#### **Threats to Banking Customers:**
- **Phishing Kits:** Improved with user-friendly management systems and sophisticated self-defense mechanisms.
- **Social Engineering Schemes:** Widely known schemes involve prompting victims to reveal logins, passwords, and bank card details via various channels (phone calls, messaging apps, social media).
- **Mobile Banking Trojans:** Advanced features like autofill and manipulation of push notifications are being used to steal financial data.

#### **Geographical Trends:**
- **US and Post-Soviet Countries:** High prevalence of cyber threats, particularly in carding activities and banking trojan usage.
- **Brazil:** Emerging as a new source of banking Trojans targeting the local population.

#### **Future Predictions:**
- **Decline in Banking Trojans:** Expected decrease in active banking Trojans for PCs, except in Brazil.
- **Rise of JS Sniffers:** Rapid evolution and increased profitability of JS sniffers compared to traditional banking Trojans.
- **Shift in Distribution Channels:** Possible shift from email to text messages as the main distribution channel for phishing links.
- **DDoS Botnets:** Potential rental of DDoS botnets built on infected routers to hackers engaging in phishing.
- **Remote Support Schemes:** Increase in schemes involving remote support through mobile devices, aimed at obtaining bank card information or requesting payment for fake services.

#### **Key Statistics:**
- **38% growth** in the total number of compromised cards.
- **$229 million fine** imposed on British Airways due to a data breach.
- **46% increase** in the number of dumps for sale.
- **19% growth** in the sale of textual data (card number, CVV, expiration date).

### Conclusion:
The report underscores the evolving nature of cyber threats, with a shift towards more sophisticated techniques like JS sniffers and social engineering. Financial institutions and customers must remain vigilant and adapt their security measures to counter these emerging threats.
