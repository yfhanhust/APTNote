While larger binary embedded payloads such as EXE and Office 97-2003 are more capable,
attackers tend to use them less often (as shown in Figure 5) because they increase the overall size
of the OneNote sample. Attackers tend to prefer a smaller overall file size, as smaller-sized
malware is easier to include in common malware delivery mechanisms such as email
attachments, thus raising less suspicion.
As illustrated in Figure 6 above, embedded malicious EXE and Office 97-2003 file payloads tend
to be larger, and embedded malicious HTA and JavaScript files tend to be smaller.
