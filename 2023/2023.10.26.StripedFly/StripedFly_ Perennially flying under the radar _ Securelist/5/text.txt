The infection
The first detected shellcode was located within the WININIT.EXE process, which has the
ability to download binary files from bitbucket[.]org and execute PowerShell scripts. At
the time of the initial detections, the infection vector was unknown. However, as our
investigation progressed, we discovered an SMBv1 exploit that was remarkably similar to
EternalBlue.
The kernel shellcode, delivered via an exploit, injects an additional shellcode into the user
space. The payload is then deployed, which includes a framework with plugin-like
expandable functionality along with an extremely lightweight TOR network client. Once
this process is completed, the gates are permanently sealed, and the malware proceeds
to disable the SMBv1 protocol on the infected system.
The worming functionality attempts to propagate within the local network, relying not only
on the exploit but also on the SSH protocol, using keys found on the victim’s machine.
