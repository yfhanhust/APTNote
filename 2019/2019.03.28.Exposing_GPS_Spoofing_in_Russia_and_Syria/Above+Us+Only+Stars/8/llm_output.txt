Geography of Detected Activity
The scope of GNSS spoofing events appears to be far more indiscriminate, persistent,
and widespread than previous public reporting suggests. In total, C4ADS found 9,883
GNSS spoofing instances affecting 1,311 vessels across ten locations in Russia, Crimea,

Sevastopol and Olyva on the Crimean Peninsula. Finally, working with researchers from

### Key Findings from the GNSS Spoofing Report

#### Introduction
The report highlights significant instances of Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) spoofing in Russia, Crimea, and Syria. These activities often involve forcing victim receivers to display false coordinates or invalid navigation signals, primarily targeting areas near sensitive government facilities and airspace.

### Main Trends Observed

1. **Multiple Spoofed Locations**
   - In several locations, vessels were spoofed to multiple airports over different periods.
   - Example: Vessels off the coast of Gelendzhik were spoofed to Gelendzhik Airport, Sochi-Adler Airport, and Vnukovo Airport in Moscow.

2. **Proximity to Sensitive Government Airspace**
   - High correlation between GNSS spoofing events and areas with sensitive or prohibited airspace.
   - Examples:
     - **Gelendzhik**: Prohibited airspace over a residence allegedly tied to President Putin.
     - **Sochi**: Adjacent to government residences operated by the Russian FSO.
     - **Central Moscow**: Near the Kremlin.
     - **Khmeimim Airbase, Syria**: Protected by advanced Russian military electronic warfare (EW) and anti-aircraft systems.

3. **Indiscriminate Nature**
   - Spoofing events affect all nearby GNSS receivers regardless of vessel nationality.
   - Example: Russia-flagged ships were among the most affected vessels across almost all locations.
   - Potential safety hazards for both maritime and air navigation, especially near Khmeimim Airbase where spoofing signals are 500 times stronger than authentic GPS signals.

- **Details**:
  - Vessels spoofed to Gelendzhik Airport, Sochi-Adler Airport, and Vnukovo Airport in Moscow.

### Implications and Concerns

1. **Safety Risks**
   - Significant safety hazards for maritime and air navigation, particularly near sensitive military and government facilities.
   - Potential violation of international regulations, such as the ITU Radio Regulations Articles, which prohibit harmful radio frequency interference.

2. **National Security Objectives**
   - Activities are likely a byproduct of efforts to enhance national security and protect sensitive areas from potential threats.
   - However, the indiscriminate nature of these activities poses broader risks that extend beyond intended targets.

### Conclusion
The report underscores the need for international cooperation and regulatory measures to address the safety and security implications of GNSS spoofing. Further research and monitoring are essential to mitigate the risks associated with these activities and ensure the integrity of global navigation systems.
